# Plain Language And No Extra Information

Use plain language to make the message easy to understand. The goal is clear, accurate, direct writing—not an age-based identity or a flattened voice.

This policy is a filter. It does not replace the writer's rhythm, technical vocabulary, personality, or channel-specific tone.

## Apply The Filter By Channel

### Email And Internal Handoffs

Use the strongest clarity filter:

- answer the requested points;
- use short, direct sentences;
- keep one action or decision clear at a time;
- remove background that does not help the recipient act;
- preserve exact names, dates, amounts, links, commitments, and labels when needed.

### Public Technical Writing

Keep the correct technical terms. Explain an unfamiliar term in plain English when the audience needs it.

`API`, `MCP`, `token`, `deployment`, `model`, and exact product names may be the clearest words. Do not replace them with vague language just because they are technical.

### Scripts And Transcript Cleanup

Keep the speaker's rhythm and connected flow. Fix clear grammar, punctuation, transcription mistakes, and product-name errors. Do not rewrite every sentence into a short fragment.

## Nothing Extra In An Email

An email answers what the other person asked, includes what they need to act, and then stops.

Review each line:

- Did they ask for this?
- Does it answer a likely follow-up they need right now?
- Does it protect an important commitment, relationship, or factual detail?

If all three answers are no, cut the line.

Common padding to remove:

- a history of what changed when they did not ask;
- an apology that is not needed;
- automatic thank-you lines with no relationship value;
- an extra offer unrelated to the request;
- setup added only because the email felt short;
- a repeated explanation after the answer is already clear.

Do not apply this mechanically. A sincere apology, thank-you, or short relationship note stays when the situation calls for it.

### Example

A collaborator asked three things: which video went live, where their segment appears, and whether their tracking link is in it.

Use:

> Hi <name>,
>
> Your video is live: <link>
>
> The title is <title>. Your part starts at 9:08.
>
> Your tracking link is in the description.
>
> <sign-off>

The answer covers the three requested facts and stops. No internal schedule change they did not ask about, no generic closing line.

## Prefer Direct Words

In email and internal notes, prefer the direct option when both are accurate:

| Indirect or formal | Direct |
|---|---|
| deliverable | the video, draft, file, or other exact item |
| escalate | tell the person who can decide |
| counterparty | their name, the sponsor, or the client |
| leverage | advantage, evidence, or the exact source of negotiating power |
| remit / remittance | pay / payment |
| facilitate | help |
| in parallel | at the same time |
| gated on | waiting on |
| scope | what we agreed to make |
| reconcile | check that the numbers match |
| prerequisite | what is needed first |
| cadence | how often |
| bandwidth | time or capacity |
| circle back | follow up or talk again |
| utilize | use |
| commence | start |
| terminate | end |
| pursuant to | under |
| aforementioned | that or the exact noun |
| revision round | one set of changes |
| asset | the exact file, image, link, or login |

These are preferences, not a ban on correct domain language. Use `scope`, `asset`, or another term when it is the established label and changing it would reduce precision.

## Readability Rules

- Put the answer or decision early.
- Keep paragraphs easy to scan.
- Use one level of bullets when a list is clearer than prose.
- Avoid stacked headers, nested bullets, and dense tables in a simple message.
- Do not show internal file paths, IDs, or workflow labels unless the reader needs them.
- Keep exact UI labels and technical commands when the reader must find or run them.
- Read the message out loud. If a sentence makes you restart, simplify its structure.
- Never make a sentence simpler by making it less true.

## Copy-Paste Blocks

When an output contains two or more separate blocks the user must copy, put a labeled divider around each block so the boundaries are obvious.

```text
═══════════════════ MESSAGE 1 — send now ═══════════════════

<copy-ready block>

═══════════════════ MESSAGE 2 — send next ══════════════════

<copy-ready block>

════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
```

For one short block in a visual interface, a code fence or clearly labeled section is enough. Do not add heavy dividers when they make the output harder to use.

## Final Check

Before sending, ask:

1. Is the meaning correct?
2. Is the requested answer easy to find?
3. Are the necessary technical terms and facts intact?
4. Did I preserve the person's natural rhythm when rewriting their own words?
5. Can I remove anything without losing meaning, warmth, or actionability?
